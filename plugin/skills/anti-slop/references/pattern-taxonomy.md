# Contextual anti-slop patterns

Use these as contextual heuristics, not a detector or ban list. A finding needs an exact citation, a reason this instance weakens the unit, and the smallest fix that preserves its meaning and voice. A strong, intentional, audience-appropriate instance is a retain.

| Pattern | Contextual signal | Minimum fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic framing | An opener delays the subject with broad setting, process, or importance language that could introduce almost any piece. | Start with the specific point or retain framing that establishes necessary context. |
| Binary contrast | A `not X, but Y` or repeated negative setup creates drama without adding a meaningful distinction. | State the useful distinction directly. |
| Faux insight | The prose announces what “people miss” or what “nobody tells you” instead of supporting the claim. | Remove the setup; keep the claim only if the unit supports it. |
| Interpretive metadiscourse | The text tells the reader that a point is key, subtle, obvious, or important rather than showing why. | Delete the instruction to react or supply the existing concrete support. |
| Unsupported attribution | An unnamed group, expert, study, or industry is used to make a claim sound settled. | Cite the source, narrow the claim, or remove it. |
| Empty intensification | A qualifier or elevated term adds status or force but no fact, mechanism, consequence, or judgment. | Remove it or replace it with a supported detail already available. |
| Abstract action | An organization, feature, or launch is said to “serve as,” “represent,” “underscore,” or “enable” where a direct actor and action are clearer. | Name what it does, who does it, or the concrete result. |
| Synonym cycling | Several terms refer to the same thing only to avoid repeating the clearest word. | Use the established term consistently. |
| Manufactured rhythm | Repeated fragment shapes, stacked short sentences, or a rule-of-three cadence draws attention to performance over the point. | Join, vary, or delete only the redundant beat. |
| Rhetorical setup | A self-answered question, “plot twist,” “think about it,” or similar reveal asks for attention before making the point. | Make the point plainly. |
| Fake-profound ending | A metaphor, aphorism, or mic-drop closing recasts a concrete conclusion as a grand lesson. | End on the strongest concrete takeaway or next action. |
| Summary recap | A closing repeats what the reader has just read without adding a decision, consequence, or action. | End earlier or retain the part that advances the purpose. |
| Decorative formatting | Styling, headings, emoji, bold emphasis, or a list creates theatrical structure rather than helping scanning or meaning. | Use the simplest structure that serves the content and its established style. |
| Punctuation as drama | Colons, dashes, or fragments manufacture a reveal or rhythm rather than clarifying grammar or a list. | Rewrite as a plain sentence; retain punctuation that improves the text in its language. |

These labels describe rhetorical effects, not literal English phrases. For a non-English unit, use the closest category only when the same effect occurs in its language and cultural context. Do not translate a pattern name into a search query or assume a direct lexical equivalent.
